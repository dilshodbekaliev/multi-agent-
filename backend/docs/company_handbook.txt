Company Handbook — Customer Success & Retention Policy

Our churn definition: a customer is marked as churned if they have not placed
an order in the last 90 days and have not renewed their subscription.

Common reasons customers churn, based on exit surveys:
1. Price sensitivity — customers on the Basic plan cite cost as the top reason
   for cancellation, especially in the first 6 months.
2. Poor onboarding — customers who do not complete the onboarding checklist
   within 14 days of signup churn at nearly triple the rate of those who do.
3. Feature gaps — mid-market customers frequently request integrations with
   third-party accounting tools; the lack of this integration is the second
   most common complaint in exit surveys.
4. Support response time — customers who wait more than 24 hours for a first
   response to a support ticket are significantly more likely to churn within
   the following quarter.

Retention playbook:
- Customers inactive for 30 days receive an automated re-engagement email.
- Customers inactive for 60 days are flagged for outreach by the customer
  success team.
- At 90 days of inactivity, the customer is marked churned in the CRM and
  routed to a win-back campaign three months later.

Regional notes:
- Central Asian accounts (Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan) show
  higher retention when onboarding is offered in the local language.
- Enterprise accounts rarely churn for price reasons; when they do churn, it
  is almost always due to a champion leaving the company.
